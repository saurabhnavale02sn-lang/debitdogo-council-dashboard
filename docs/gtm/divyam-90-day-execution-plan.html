Rule: a Happenstance hit never enters an automated sequence. Burning a warm path with a templated message is the most expensive mistake in this plan.

Sandeep's ask, made concrete: I'd run the network query across all 50 accounts in week 1, then bring a ranked list of ~10 intro requests with the message pre-drafted. His cost is forwarding an email, not thinking of names.

3.2 LinkedIn — SalesRobot, Tier 2

StepDayActionCeiling
1D1Connection request, no note for cold profiles20–25 / day
2D3 after acceptValue message — one observation about their AI feature. No ask—
3D6The disqualifying question—
4D10Artifact — MakeMyTrip teardown or a published benchmark—

Safety discipline. Stay at 20–25 connects a day even though the tool allows more. Warm the account for two weeks before automating. Never run SalesRobot and manual outreach on the same profile on the same day. A restricted profile costs a month of pipeline.

Where AI personalisation helps, and where it doesn't: SalesRobot's icebreakers drawn from recent posts are fine for step 1. Steps 2 and 3 carry a technical claim and get written by me — an AI icebreaker that misreads an engineering post is worse than no personalisation at all.

3.4 Tier 1 — no automation at all

Fifteen accounts. Hand-written, one at a time, with a modelled inference-spend estimate in the first email. Roughly 25 minutes each. These are the accounts that become the case studies.

Where this plan breaks first — pilot capacity. If engineering can support only two concurrent pilots, the honest 90-day number is two logos, not three. I'd rather pace outbound than stack up pilots we can't service. That is question 2 for you.

The rule that makes this work: the champion presents, not me. A number defended by their own engineer survives a budget meeting. A vendor deck does not.

Success metric wording that works: "≥30% reduction in blended cost per request on [workload], with rubric score within 2% of baseline, measured over 14 days of production traffic."
