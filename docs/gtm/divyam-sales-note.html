What Divyam Sells, and How I Would Sell It

What this document is. Three pages. Page one is the problem we sell into. Page two is what Divyam does about it. Page three is how I would go and find the buyers. No technical detail — this is written the way I would explain it to a customer.

01The problem, in five lines

  • Companies run their AI features on the most expensive models available.
  • Most of what they send is routine work — lookups, sorting, formatting, summarising.
  • A cheaper model would handle that routine work just as well.
  • Nobody switches, because nobody can prove the quality will hold.
  • So the bill keeps growing, and their data keeps leaving the building to get processed.

02Why "sending it outside" stopped being acceptable

  • Research, financial documents and customer records leave the company to be processed.
  • This year the AI labs themselves disclosed incidents where their own models reached systems they were never meant to touch.
  • The risk stopped being theoretical, and buyers noticed.
  • For banks and universities it is no longer a preference. It is a rule they have to follow.

03The Navier–Stokes point you raised

  • Navier–Stokes is the famous maths problem that cannot be solved on paper — you can only measure the system as it moves and keep correcting.
  • Choosing which model should answer which request is the same kind of problem. There is no formula. Model rankings and price lists will not tell you.
  • Which is why competitors get it wrong: they hand the customer a set of fixed rules, written by a person, on a day. New models arrive weekly, so those rules are out of date almost immediately.
The sentence I would use with a customer: “Everyone else gives you rules that are stale the day they are written. Divyam measures your actual traffic and corrects itself — continuously.”

04What Divyam actually does

How it works — three steps
1. Learn their standardDivyam studies the customer’s own examples to learn what a good answer looks like for them — not a generic score.
→
2. Send work to the cheapest model that passesEvery single request is matched to the cheapest model that still meets that standard. Hard requests still go to the expensive model.
→
3. Keep checking, keep correctingNew model arrives, traffic changes, quality drifts — Divyam re-tests and adjusts on its own.
Where the saving comes from — the work mix, estimated
Routine work — roughly three quarters
Genuinely hard — the rest
Today all of this runs on the expensive model. Divyam moves the left-hand block to cheaper models and leaves the right-hand block alone. Estimated outcome: roughly half the cost, with no change to the product and no drop in quality.
  • It runs inside the customer’s own environment — nothing leaves their building.
  • Nothing moves unless quality holds. The standard is the gate, not a hope.
  • No engineering work for the customer — the product stays exactly as it is.
  • Already proven. MakeMyTrip cut their AI cost by more than half on a live product with no quality loss.

05The GPU partnership — a second route to market

  • Tie-ups with Indian GPU providers such as Yotta and E2E, including a place on their marketplace.
  • They sell the hardware. Customers often do not know what is genuinely worth running on it.
  • Divyam makes that hardware useful by proving the cheaper open models are good enough to rely on.
  • Their sales teams reach buyers who have already spent the money — that is distribution we do not pay for.
  • Especially useful for universities, who often buy their compute from exactly these providers.

06How I would split my time

2 days a week · ~20 institutions a month 3 days a week · ~30 companies a month

Why universities get 40%

  • Large numbers of users in a single institution.
  • Keeping data in-house is written policy, not preference.
  • Tight budgets make “half the cost” an easy conversation.
  • Nobody else is calling on them.
  • Students become users who carry it into industry later.

Why corporate stays 60%

  • Shorter decisions, tied to a quarter.
  • This is where revenue lands inside the funding window.
  • Bigger, faster deals once a single workload is proven.
  • Produces the reference names universities respect.
